-ing vs to+verb

I enjoy swimming OR I want to swim?

El dilema más común: cuándo usar gerundio o infinitivo.

El Gran Dilema

Cuando un verbo va seguido de otro verbo, ¿cuál usas? La respuesta depende del PRIMER verbo. Algunos requieren gerundio (-ing), otros infinitivo (to + verb), y otros aceptan ambos.

Estructura

  • Gerundio: verb + ing
  • Infinitivo: to + verb
  • Bare infinitive: verb (sin to)

Verbos seguidos de GERUNDIO (-ing)

Estos verbos siempre se siguen con verb-ing.

enjoy — I enjoy reading.
avoid — She avoids driving at night.
finish — I finished cleaning.
mind — Do you mind waiting?
suggest — He suggested going out.
practice — She practices singing.
keep (= continuar) — Keep trying.
consider — I'm considering moving.
admit — He admitted stealing.
deny — She denied cheating.
imagine — Imagine winning!
recommend — I recommend visiting Paris.

Verbos seguidos de INFINITIVO (to + verb)

Estos verbos siempre se siguen con to + verb.

want — I want to go home.
need — I need to sleep.
decide — She decided to leave.
hope — I hope to see you.
plan — We plan to travel.
learn — I'm learning to drive.
refuse — He refused to help.
promise — I promise to call.
agree — They agreed to come.
offer — She offered to pay.
seem — He seems to know.
manage — I managed to finish.

Verbos que aceptan AMBOS (sin cambio de significado)

Con estos puedes usar gerundio o infinitivo, sin cambio de significado.

love: I love swimming / I love to swim.
like: I like cooking / I like to cook.
hate: She hates cleaning / She hates to clean.
prefer: I prefer walking / I prefer to walk.
start: It started raining / It started to rain.
begin: He began talking / He began to talk.
continue: She continued reading / continued to read.

CUIDADO: Cambian de significado

Con estos verbos, gerundio o infinitivo cambian totalmente el significado:

REMEMBER

• I remember locking the door. (lo hice y lo recuerdo)

• I remembered to lock the door. (me acordé y lo hice)

FORGET

• I'll never forget meeting her. (la conocí, no lo olvido)

• Don't forget to call. (¡no se te olvide!)

STOP

• He stopped smoking. (dejó de fumar)

• He stopped to smoke. (paró para fumar)

TRY

• Try calling him. (experimenta, prueba)

• Try to call him. (haz el esfuerzo)

REGRET

• I regret telling you. (ya te dije, me arrepiento)

• I regret to tell you... (formal: lamento informarte)

MEAN

• This means working harder. (implica)

• I mean to help. (intento)

Patrón: verb + objeto + infinitivo

Algunos verbos requieren un objeto (persona) antes del infinitivo.

She told me to wait.
He asked me to help.
They expect us to succeed.
I want you to come.
She invited us to dinner.
My boss allowed me to leave early.
The doctor advised him to rest.
Mom warned me not to touch it.

Bare Infinitive (sin TO)

Después de make / let / help y los modales, no se usa "to".

Con make / let / help

  • She made me laugh.
  • Let me think.
  • He helped me finish.

Con modales

  • You should go now.
  • I can swim.
  • She must study.
