Modal verbs en pasado: especular, criticar y deducir.

Modales en Pasado

Los modal perfects son la forma de los modales en pasado. Sirven para especular, criticar, deducir o expresar arrepentimiento sobre acciones pasadas.

Fórmula Universal

Modal + have + Past Participle

Ej: must have gone, should have called

MUST HAVE — Certeza casi total (pasado)

Para expresar una conclusión casi segura sobre algo pasado.

  • He's not here. He must have left already. (estoy seguro)
  • The ground is wet. It must have rained.
  • She looks tired. She must have worked all night.
  • You must have studied hard to pass!

CAN'T HAVE / COULDN'T HAVE — Imposible (pasado)

Para expresar que algo era imposible en el pasado.

  • She can't have seen him — he was in Tokyo!
  • He couldn't have stolen it — he was with me.
  • They can't have left already, it's only 5 PM.
  • You couldn't have known; nobody told you.

MAY / MIGHT / COULD HAVE — Posibilidad pasada

Para especular sobre algo que quizás pasó.

  • She might have forgotten our meeting.
  • He may have lost his phone.
  • They could have taken the wrong train.
  • The package might have arrived while we were out.

Los tres son prácticamente intercambiables: ~30-50% de probabilidad.

SHOULD HAVE — Crítica / Arrepentimiento

Para expresar que algo era lo correcto pero NO se hizo (o se hizo cuando no se debía).

SHOULD HAVE + done it (no lo hice, debí hacerlo)

  • I should have studied more. (no estudié)
  • You should have told me.
  • We should have left earlier.

SHOULDN'T HAVE + done it (lo hice, no debí)

  • I shouldn't have eaten so much. (me sentí mal)
  • You shouldn't have spoken to her like that.
  • We shouldn't have trusted him.

WOULD HAVE — Hipótesis pasada (3er condicional)

Para hablar de algo que habría pasado bajo otras circunstancias.

  • I would have helped, but I was busy.
  • She would have come if you'd invited her.
  • We would have won with a better team.
  • He wouldn't have said that on purpose.
